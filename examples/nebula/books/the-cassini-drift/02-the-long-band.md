title: The Long Band
label: Log 2
---

*Survey tender Cassini. Log 2. Filed on the long band. Transit time to the
archive: eleven months, four days.*

Three hundred and eleven plates of the same field. The knot moved.

Not much — a little over two arcseconds across the whole series, which at the
distance the parallax gives works out to a speed I have now recomputed nine
times because the first eight answers were all the same and I did not like any
of them. It is not drifting. It is falling. There is something at the eastern
end of LA-2211 with a mass I cannot account for from the light, and the knot is
going round it.

I sent the plates on the narrow band. The narrow band gave up at plate ninety.
The transmitter is not broken; there is simply more data here than this tender
was built to talk about, and the difference has to go out on the long band,
which is a phrase that means: it goes at the speed of a slow ship, in a crate,
and somebody unpacks it a year later.

> **Marek (04:12):** If you are reading this, the crate arrived.
> **Marek (04:13):** I am, by your calendar, eleven months out of date and still looking at the same square of sky.

---

That is the part nobody tells you about an archive. It is not a record of what
happened. It is a record of what got here.

Somebody will open the crate and put plate 311 on the light table and see a
smudge move two arcseconds, and it will take them ten minutes, and it took me
eleven months and three hundred and eleven exposures and one very bad week in
the fourth month when the attitude control gave out and I hand-corrected the
mount for nine days.

Ten minutes is the right amount of time for it to take. That is what the
archive is *for.* I would only ask that the ten minutes be spent.

Provisional designation LA-2211 stands. Recommend a proper instrument, and
somebody younger.

*End of log.*
