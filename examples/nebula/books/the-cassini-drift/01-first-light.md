title: First Light
label: Log 1
---

*Survey tender Cassini. Log 1. Filed on the narrow band, receipt confirmed.*

The plate came out of the developer at 04:12 and I have been looking at it
since. I am aware that this is not the sort of thing that goes in a log. I am
putting it in the log anyway, because there is nobody else aboard to tell, and
because in ninety years somebody in an archive is going to want to know what
the first one looked like.

It looked like nothing. That is the honest answer. Sixteen hours of exposure on
a glass plate the size of a dinner tray, and what came up out of the emulsion
was a grey field with a smear in the lower third that could have been a
thumbprint. I checked it against the dark frame twice.

Then I put the loupe on it and the smear had structure. Filaments. Two of them,
braided, running about eleven arcminutes with a knot at the eastern end where
something is either forming or has already finished forming and gone quiet.

---

The catalogue wants a designation and a bearing and a plate number, and it will
get all three. What the catalogue has no column for is that the knot is the
exact colour of a lit window seen from a road at night, and that I sat with the
loupe for forty minutes before I remembered to write anything down.

Provisional designation LA-2211. Plate 004. Bearing filed separately.

I am going to expose the same field again tomorrow, and the day after, and every
day the tender's attitude control will hold still for it. If the knot moves,
that is a body. If it brightens, that is an event. If it does neither, it is
sixteen hours a day for nothing, and I will file that too.

*End of log.*
