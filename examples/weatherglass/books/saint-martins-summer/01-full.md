*Eleventh of November. Wind light, backing easterly. Glass steady at 30.3.*

Every country that has weather has a name for this: a handful of mild days
dropped into the middle of November, after the first frosts have already been
and gone and everyone has resigned themselves. The English call it Saint
Martin's summer, the Americans an Indian summer, the Swedes Brittsommar, and
the French *l'été de la Saint-Martin*, which is the same saint again and the
better phrase.

What it actually is, is a blocking high. The jet stream, which spends most of
the autumn firing depressions at us one after another like a man dealing cards,
gets a great slow anticyclone parked in its way and has to go round. For four
days, sometimes ten, nothing arrives. The air stands still and warms in the
sun, and the nights go clear and cold because there is nothing overhead to keep
the day's heat in.

---

The Society's position, on the record, is that it is not a summer and not a
reprieve. It is a gap in the traffic.

But the roses put out a second flush, and the bees are up again on the ivy, and
last Thursday a man in the lane took his coat off and carried it, which is the
only measurement that has ever mattered. Records are what we are for; so are the
things that will not go in the column.

*End of entry.*
