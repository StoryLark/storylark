*MUSIC: the theme, brought up and held under.*

*ANNOUNCER: The Wireless Playhouse presents — "The Signal at Dunmore." A serial
in two parts. Part one: The Listening Post.*

*MUSIC: out. Wind. A gate banging somewhere off.*

The listening post at Dunmore Head is a hut, and calling it a station is the
sort of thing the Ministry does on paper. Two rooms, a stove that draws badly,
an aerial guyed to the rock with wire that sings in anything over a force five,
and a receiver older than the man operating it.

The man operating it is Corporal Reece, who is nineteen, and whose entire
training consisted of a fortnight at Bletchley learning to write down what he
heard without deciding first whether it meant anything.

That turns out to be the whole job. He writes down what he hears. Once a week a
motorcycle comes up the track and takes the book away, and a fresh book is left,
and nobody ever tells him what was in the last one.

> **REECE (2140):** Dunmore listening post, night watch. Band's quiet. Trawler traffic on four-eight-zero, nothing else moving.
> **REECE (2202):** Correction. Something on six-one-two.
> **REECE (2203):** Six-one-two is not allocated.

---

It is a woman's voice, and she is reading numbers.

She reads them the way a person reads a shopping list, without weight, in
groups of five, and then she stops, and then twenty minutes later she starts
again from the beginning. Reece writes down four hundred and forty numbers
before his hand cramps and the carrier drops out and the band goes back to
being the sea.

He notes the time — twenty-two hundred and two — because that is what the book
is for.

The next night she comes up at twenty-two hundred and two.

The night after that, twenty-two hundred and two.

By the Friday, Reece is at the set from half past nine with the kettle already
boiled, and he has stopped thinking of it as traffic, which is the first
mistake, and he has started thinking of it as *her*, which is the second.
