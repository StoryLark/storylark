*MUSIC: the theme. Under, and out.*

*ANNOUNCER: The Wireless Playhouse concludes "The Signal at Dunmore." Part two:
Static.*

*SOUND: wind. The stove ticking.*

The motorcycle came up the track on the Tuesday, out of turn, and the man who
got off it was not the usual corporal. He wanted the book, and then he wanted
the book from the week before, and then he wanted to sit in the hut for an hour
with the door shut and Reece outside in the wind.

> **VISITOR (1120):** How long have you had her?
> **REECE (1120):** Eleven nights, sir.
> **VISITOR (1121):** And you wrote all of it down.
> **REECE (1121):** That's the job, sir.
> **VISITOR (1122):** It is. It's also the reason I've come up a hundred and forty miles on a Tuesday.

He would not say what the numbers were. He said only that they were not being
sent from anywhere the Ministry had a name for, and that the equipment required
to send them at that power did not, so far as anybody could establish, exist.

Then he said the thing Reece has not been able to put down since:

> **VISITOR (1140):** Nobody's transmitting, Corporal. We've had three stations triangulate her. The lines don't cross at a place. They cross at a *time.*

---

*SOUND: the receiver. Carrier, and then the voice, mid-group.*

Reece kept the watch for another six weeks. She came up at twenty-two hundred
and two every night, and read her numbers, and stopped, and on the twelfth of
March she did not stop — she got to the end of the groups and there was a pause
of about four seconds, and then, in the same flat shopping-list voice, she read
out an address in Portsmouth, and a date eleven years in the future, and the
carrier dropped.

The hut at Dunmore Head is still there. The aerial came down in the eighties.
The books went to a repository in Middlesex and were destroyed, along with a
great deal else, in a fire that the file describes as accidental and that
somebody has, at some point, underlined.

*ANNOUNCER: You have been listening to "The Signal at Dunmore," a Wireless
