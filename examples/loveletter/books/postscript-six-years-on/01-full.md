label: The reply
---

*Written on the back of an envelope, in a house that was being cleared.*

Dear A.,

The desk went to me, which I think surprised everybody including the desk. I
was not looking for anything. I was looking for the little brass key to the
front room, and instead there was a drawer that had swollen shut in some damp
winter and had to be persuaded with a chisel, and in it, four things: a
chequebook from a bank that no longer exists, a pressed something that was
probably once a flower, a receipt, and you.

I read it standing up, which is the wrong way to read a letter. Then I sat down
on the floor of a house I no longer live in and read it again properly.

---

You should know that the telephone was, in fact, working. That is the detail I
cannot get past. Not the platform, not the umbrella — the *telephone*, sitting
in the hall for six years, in full working order, an arm's length from both of
us at various times.

I am not going to pretend I would have said the right thing. I was
twenty-seven. I had the emotional range of a filing cabinet and half the
storage. If you had rung me that Friday I would very likely have said something
about the tap.

But I have had six years to draft this, which is an unfair advantage, and I
intend to use it:

I turned round on the platform because I had already decided. I have never once
had to wonder whether you were still there. That is the whole of it, and it
took me a drawer and a chisel to be able to write it down.

I am posting this one.

T.

*End of letter.*
